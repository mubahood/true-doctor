# Data processing agreement

Version {dpa_version}. Prepared for {clinic} on {date}.

## 1. The parties

- **The controller:** {clinic}, {clinic_address} ("the clinic"). The clinic decides what personal data it collects about its patients and staff, why, and for how long.
- **The processor:** {processor}, trading as {product} ("we"). We run the online service the clinic uses and process personal data only on the clinic's behalf.

This agreement is made under the Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 of Uganda ("the Act") and its Regulations. It applies while the clinic uses the online service and for as long afterwards as we hold any of its data.

## 2. What we process, and why

| What | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Providing the hospital management service the clinic subscribed to: registering patients, recording visits and care, orders and results, pharmacy, admissions, billing, insurance, reports, and messages to patients the clinic switches on. |
| People | The clinic's patients (and their next of kin and guarantors), and the clinic's staff. |
| Data | Identity and contact details; health records (special personal data under the Act); billing, insurance and payment records; staff accounts and the audit trail of what they did. |
| Duration | The subscription, then the grace period before the clinic's data is deleted (section 9). |

## 3. Our obligations

We will:

1. process the clinic's personal data only on its documented instructions, which are this agreement and the clinic's use of the service. If an instruction would break the Act, we will say so;
2. never use the data for our own purposes: no selling, no advertising, no training of models, no profiling;
3. keep each clinic's data separate from every other clinic's at the database layer;
4. make sure everyone at {processor} who can reach the data is bound to confidentiality and reaches it only to support the clinic;
5. keep the data secure (section 5);
6. help the clinic answer its patients' requests to see, correct or object to the use of their data (section 6);
7. tell the clinic without undue delay, and in any case within 24 hours, after we become aware of a breach affecting its data (section 7);
8. help the clinic with any data protection impact assessment and with questions from the Personal Data Protection Office;
9. delete or return the data at the end of the service (section 9);
10. keep a record of the processing we do for the clinic, and make the information needed to show compliance with this agreement available to the clinic on request.

## 4. Sub-processors

We use a small number of suppliers to run the service: the hosting provider for the servers and backups, an email delivery service, an SMS provider for messages the clinic switches on, and a payment provider for online payments. Each receives only what it needs (an SMS provider receives a phone number and the message text; a payment provider receives an amount and a reference, never a clinical record). We will tell the clinic before adding or replacing a sub-processor, and the clinic may object.

Where a supplier processes data outside Uganda, we will only use it where the Act's conditions for transfer abroad are met.

## 5. Security

The measures in place include: per-hospital separation of every record; role-based access; two-step sign-in for administrators; encryption of card numbers and bank details at rest; encrypted connections; an audit trail of changes to patient and visit records; nightly encrypted backups kept off the server, with a restore tested every month; and monitoring of errors and availability. We review these measures when the service changes.

## 6. Patients' requests

Requests from patients come to the clinic, which answers them. The service gives the clinic what it needs: a copy of everything held about a patient (as a PDF and a data file), correction through the normal edit with the change logged, the consent switches for messages, and a log of each request and its answer. If a patient contacts us directly, we will pass the request to the clinic and not answer it ourselves.

## 7. Breaches

If we become aware of a breach affecting the clinic's data, we will tell the clinic's administrator within 24 hours, with what we know: what happened, what data and how many people are affected, what we have done to contain it, and who to contact. We will help the clinic notify the Personal Data Protection Office and the people affected, which the Act requires of the controller without delay.

## 8. Audits

The clinic may ask for the information needed to show we comply with this agreement, and may ask questions about our security in writing. We will answer within 30 days.

## 9. End of the service

The clinic may export all its data at any time from Settings, Data protection: one file per table and every uploaded document. When the subscription ends, the data is kept for a grace period so the clinic can export it, then deleted from the live system; copies in backups are deleted as the backups rotate out.

## 10. Liability and law

Each party is responsible for its own compliance with the Act. This agreement is governed by the laws of Uganda and forms part of the clinic's subscription terms.

## Acceptance

The clinic accepts this agreement in Settings, Data protection. The system records the version, the date and the name of the person who accepted it.
