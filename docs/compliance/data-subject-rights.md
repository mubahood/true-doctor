# Patients' rights procedure

How {clinic} answers a patient who asks to see, correct, or stop a use of their personal data, under the Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019.

## Who does what

- **Anyone at the reception** may receive a request, by voice, on paper, by phone or by email. Write down who asked, what they asked and the date.
- **The administrator** (a user who can open Settings) logs it in the system under **Data requests**, answers it, and closes it.
- **The person in charge of data protection** at the clinic decides any refusal.

## Step by step

1. **Log it the same day.** Data requests, Log a request: the patient, who asked (the patient, or a parent or guardian for a child), what they asked, and the date received. The system sets the date the answer is due: {response_days} days later.
2. **Check who is asking.** Ask for the patient's ID, or the clinic card, before giving out anything. For a child, check the person is the parent or guardian. For anyone else, ask for the patient's written consent.
3. **Answer it** as below.
4. **Close it** in the system: what was done, or why it was refused. The log is your proof that the clinic answered.

## To see their data (access)

Open the request and choose **Their data (PDF)**. This is everything the clinic holds about the patient: details, visits, diagnoses, prescriptions, results, admissions, bills, payments, consents and messages, and who recorded each. Print it or send it to them securely. **As a data file** gives the same in a form another system can read. It is free.

## To correct their data (correction)

Open their record and correct it through the normal edit. The change, and who made it, is kept in the record's history. Do not delete what a clinician wrote in a visit note: add a correcting note instead. Tell the patient what was changed.

## To stop a use (objection)

Usually this is text messages. Switch off the consent on the patient's record (or the patient replies STOP, which does it for them). Care itself cannot be refused on the record, but the patient may decline to be contacted.

## To delete their data (deletion)

Medical and financial records must be kept for the periods in the retention schedule, so they usually cannot be deleted. Explain this to the patient and record it as refused with that reason. Anything that is not part of the medical or financial record (for example a duplicate registration) can be removed.

## When to refuse

Refuse only when the person cannot prove who they are, when the information would harm the patient or someone else, or when the law requires the record to be kept. Always give the reason.

## Time limit

Answer without delay, and within {response_days} days. The list of data requests marks an open request as **Overdue** once its date has passed.
