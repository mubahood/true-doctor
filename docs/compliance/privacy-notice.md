# Privacy notice for patients

## How {clinic} looks after your information

{clinic} keeps a record of your health and your care so that we can treat you safely, today and next time. This notice explains what we keep, why, who sees it, and what you can ask of us. It follows the Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019 of Uganda.

## What we keep

- **Who you are:** your name, date of birth, sex, phone number, address and next of kin.
- **Your health:** what you came for, your vital signs, examinations, diagnoses, prescriptions, test results, scans, admissions and treatment.
- **Payments:** your bills, what you paid and how, and your insurance or card details if you use them.

Information about your health is special personal data under the law. We collect only what we need to care for you.

## Why we keep it

- To diagnose and treat you, and to keep your care safe and continuous.
- To bill you or your insurer for your care.
- To meet our legal duties, including reports the Ministry of Health requires (these are numbers, not names).
- To send you reminders and receipts by text message, **only if you agree**. You can stop them at any time: reply STOP, or tell the reception.

## Who sees it

- The staff who care for you, each seeing only what their job needs. What they do is recorded.
- Your insurer, only what it needs to pay a claim you make through it.
- The company that runs our records system, which keeps it secure on our behalf and may not use it for anything else.
- Nobody else, unless the law requires it or you ask us to.

## How long we keep it

Medical records are kept for 10 years after your last visit (for a child, until they turn 25 if that is later). Bills and payments are kept for 5 years, as tax law requires. After that, records are deleted or made anonymous.

## Your rights

You may ask us to:

- **see** the information we hold about you, and have a copy;
- **correct** anything that is wrong or incomplete;
- **stop** using it for a purpose you object to, such as text messages;
- **delete** it, where the law allows. Medical records must be kept for the period above, so this is usually not possible while that period lasts; we will explain why.

Ask at the reception or contact us at {clinic_contact}. We will answer within {response_days} days, free of charge.

If you are not happy with our answer, you may complain to the Personal Data Protection Office (part of the National Information Technology Authority, Uganda).
