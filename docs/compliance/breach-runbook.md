# Data breach procedure

What {clinic} does when personal data is lost, stolen, changed without permission, or seen by someone who should not have seen it. Examples: a laptop or phone with the system on it is stolen; a password is shared or guessed; a printout of a patient's results is left in a public place; records are sent to the wrong person.

## Who decides

The person in charge of data protection at the clinic leads, with the clinic's administrator. Write their names here: ______________________ and ______________________.

## In the first hour: contain it

1. **Stop it continuing.** Change the password of any account involved. In the system: Users, deactivate the account, or ask the person to sign out of every session. For a lost device: Offline devices, revoke it, so its copy can no longer sync.
2. **Recover what you can.** Ask for the printout or the email back; ask the wrong recipient to delete it and confirm in writing.
3. **Write down what you know**, with times: what happened, when it was found, who found it, which records and how many patients, and what you have done.
4. **Tell us** (the system's support: {processor_email}) if the system itself may be involved. We will check the audit trail and the server, and help.

## Within a day: assess it

- What data: names and contacts only, or health records (special personal data)?
- How many people?
- Could it harm them: discrimination, fraud, distress, danger?
- Is it still exposed?

## Without delay: notify

The Act (section 23) requires the clinic to notify the **Personal Data Protection Office** immediately after a breach is discovered, with what happened, the data and people affected, and what has been done. Notify within 72 hours at the latest. If it is not clear yet, notify with what you know and add the rest later.

**Tell the people affected** when the breach could harm them, in plain words: what happened, what data, what the clinic has done, what they can do, and who to contact. Do it by phone, message or letter, whichever reaches them.

Keep a copy of every notice sent.

## Afterwards

Record the breach in a breach log (date, what happened, people affected, notifications sent, what changed). Fix the cause: a training session, a new rule, a change in who can see what. Review this procedure.

## Practise it

Once a year, walk through this procedure with a pretend case (for example, a stolen reception laptop). Note what was slow or unclear and fix it.
